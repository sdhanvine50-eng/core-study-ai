# CHAPTER INTRODUCTION

Plants are living organisms that can detect and respond to changes in their surroundings.

Although plants do not have a nervous system like animals, they are able to respond to stimuli through:

- Growth responses.
- Changes in cell growth.
- Changes in cell turgor.
- Chemical signals.
- Plant hormones or phytohormones.

Examples of stimuli that plants respond to include:

- Light.
- Gravity.
- Water.
- Touch.
- Chemicals.
- Temperature.

Plant responses allow plants to:

1. Obtain sufficient light for photosynthesis.
2. Grow in an appropriate direction.
3. Obtain water and mineral salts.
4. Avoid or tolerate environmental stress.
5. Coordinate growth and development.
6. Reproduce successfully.
7. Respond to agricultural treatments.

The major sections of this chapter are:

1. Types of responses in plants.
2. Phytohormones.
3. Applications of phytohormones in agriculture.

---

# 5.1 TYPES OF RESPONSES

## 5.1.1 Introduction to Plant Responses

A stimulus is a change in the internal or external environment that can be detected by an organism and produces a response.

Plants detect stimuli using specialised cells or tissues and respond through changes in growth, cell activity or movement.

Plant responses can generally be divided into:

1. Tropisms.
2. Nastic responses.

---

# 5.1.2 TROPISMS

A tropism is a directional growth response of a plant part towards or away from a stimulus.

The direction of the response depends on the direction of the stimulus.

The main types of tropism are:

1. Phototropism.
2. Geotropism or gravitropism.
3. Hydrotropism.
4. Thigmotropism.
5. Chemotropism.

A tropism can be:

### Positive tropism

Growth towards the stimulus.

### Negative tropism

Growth away from the stimulus.

---

# 5.1.3 PHOTOTROPISM

Phototropism is the growth response of a plant towards or away from light.

The main plant organ involved is the shoot.

### Positive Phototropism

A shoot usually shows positive phototropism.

The shoot grows towards the light source.

This allows leaves to receive more light for photosynthesis.

### Negative Phototropism

Roots commonly show a negative response to light.

They generally grow away from light.

This supports their growth into the soil, where they can obtain water and mineral salts.

---

# 5.1.4 MECHANISM OF PHOTOTROPISM

Auxin is an important phytohormone involved in phototropism.

Consider a shoot receiving light from one side.

### Step 1

Light is detected by the shoot tip.

### Step 2

Auxin is redistributed towards the shaded side of the shoot.

### Step 3

A higher concentration of auxin develops on the shaded side.

### Step 4

In shoots, auxin stimulates cell elongation.

### Step 5

Cells on the shaded side elongate more rapidly than cells on the illuminated side.

### Step 6

The shoot bends towards the light.

This produces positive phototropism.

### Simplified sequence

Light from one side  
↓  
Auxin accumulates on shaded side  
↓  
Greater cell elongation on shaded side  
↓  
Unequal growth  
↓  
Shoot bends towards light

---

# 5.1.5 WHY DOES THE SHOOT BEND?

The shoot does not bend because auxin physically pushes it towards the light.

Instead, the bending occurs because cells on one side grow faster than cells on the other side.

This unequal growth causes the shoot to curve.

**Important exam phrase:**

"Unequal distribution of auxin causes unequal cell elongation on the two sides of the shoot."

---

# 5.1.6 GEOTROPISM / GRAVITROPISM

Geotropism, also called gravitropism, is a growth response to gravity.

### Positive geotropism

Growth towards the direction of gravity.

Roots generally show positive geotropism.

Roots grow downwards.

### Negative geotropism

Growth away from the direction of gravity.

Shoots generally show negative geotropism.

Shoots grow upwards.

### Biological importance

Root growth towards gravity helps roots penetrate the soil.

This allows roots to:

- Anchor the plant.
- Absorb water.
- Absorb mineral ions.

Shoot growth away from gravity helps stems and leaves grow towards the aerial environment where light is available.

---

# 5.1.7 MECHANISM OF ROOT GRAVITROPISM

Root gravitropism involves gravity sensing and redistribution of auxin.

The root tip contains specialised cells that help detect gravity.

When a root is placed horizontally:

1. Gravity causes auxin to become redistributed towards the lower side.
2. In roots, a relatively high concentration of auxin inhibits cell elongation.
3. Cells on the upper side elongate more rapidly.
4. Unequal growth occurs.
5. The root bends downwards.

### Important comparison

Auxin generally:

- Promotes cell elongation in shoots.
- At sufficiently high concentrations, inhibits cell elongation in roots.

This difference is extremely important in understanding plant tropisms.

---

# 5.1.8 HYDROTROPISM

Hydrotropism is the growth response of a plant towards or away from water or a moisture gradient.

Roots generally show positive hydrotropism.

Roots grow towards regions with greater water availability.

### Biological importance

Positive hydrotropism helps roots:

- Locate water in the soil.
- Improve water absorption.
- Support plant survival during periods of limited water availability.

### Example

If one side of the soil around a plant is more moist than another, growing roots may show a tendency to grow towards the region with greater moisture.

---

# 5.1.9 THIGMOTROPISM

Thigmotropism is a directional growth response to touch or physical contact.

It is especially important in climbing plants.

### Example

A tendril comes into contact with a support.

The cells on different sides of the tendril grow at different rates.

This causes the tendril to coil around the support.

### Biological importance

Thigmotropism allows climbing plants to:

- Obtain physical support.
- Grow upwards.
- Reach regions with greater light availability.
- Reduce the need to develop thick supporting stems.

---

# 5.1.10 CHEMOTROPISM

Chemotropism is a directional growth response to a chemical stimulus.

A well-known example occurs during plant reproduction.

The pollen tube grows towards the ovule in response to chemical signals released by tissues of the female reproductive structure.

This helps the male gamete reach the female gamete for fertilisation.

### Biological importance

Chemotropism helps coordinate growth in response to chemical signals.

---

# 5.1.11 SUMMARY OF TROPISMS

| Type | Stimulus | Typical response | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phototropism | Light | Shoot grows towards light | Shoot bends towards window |
| Geotropism/gravitropism | Gravity | Roots grow downwards | Root growth |
| Hydrotropism | Water | Roots grow towards water | Root growth in moist soil |
| Thigmotropism | Touch | Growth towards/around support | Tendril coiling |
| Chemotropism | Chemicals | Growth towards chemical signal | Pollen tube growth |

---

# 5.1.12 NASTIC RESPONSES

A nastic response is a plant response in which the direction of the response does not depend on the direction of the stimulus.

This differs from tropism.

Nastic responses often involve changes in cell turgor rather than directional growth.

Examples include:

- Seismonasty.
- Nyctinasty.

---

# 5.1.13 SEISMONASTY

Seismonasty is a nastic response to mechanical stimuli such as:

- Touch.
- Vibration.
- Shaking.

### Example: Mimosa pudica

When the leaves of Mimosa pudica are touched:

1. The stimulus is detected.
2. Electrical and chemical signals are transmitted through the plant.
3. Cells in specialised structures called pulvini lose turgor.
4. The leaflets fold.
5. The leaf appears to close.

This response can help reduce damage or discourage herbivores.

### Important

The response is not a directional growth response.

Therefore, it is a nastic response rather than a tropism.

---

# 5.1.14 NYCTINASTY

Nyctinasty is a nastic movement associated with the daily cycle of light and darkness.

Some plants show changes in leaf or flower position between day and night.

For example:

- Leaves may fold or droop at night.
- Some flowers open during the day and close at night.

These movements may involve changes in turgor pressure in specialised motor cells.

---

# 5.1.15 TROPISM VS NASTIC RESPONSE

| Feature | Tropism | Nastic response |
|---|---|---|
| Direction depends on stimulus direction? | Yes | No |
| Common mechanism | Directional growth | Growth or turgor changes |
| Usually slow or fast? | Often relatively slow | Some can be rapid |
| Example | Phototropism | Seismonasty |
| Typical stimulus | Light, gravity, water, touch, chemicals | Touch, vibration, day/night cycle |

---

# 5.1.16 IMPORTANCE OF PLANT RESPONSES

Plant responses are essential for survival.

### Light

Shoots grow towards light to improve photosynthesis.

### Gravity

Roots grow into soil while shoots grow upwards.

### Water

Roots grow towards water sources.

### Touch

Climbing plants use supports to grow upwards.

### Chemicals

Pollen tubes respond to chemical signals during reproduction.

### Mechanical stimulation

Some plants rapidly change leaf position when touched.

# 5.2 PHYTOHORMONES

## 5.2.1 Definition of Phytohormones

Phytohormones are naturally occurring chemical substances produced by plants that regulate growth, development and physiological processes.

They are effective in very small concentrations.

Phytohormones can be produced in one part of a plant and transported to another part.

They can affect:

- Cell division.
- Cell elongation.
- Cell differentiation.
- Root growth.
- Shoot growth.
- Flowering.
- Fruit development.
- Fruit ripening.
- Seed germination.
- Dormancy.
- Leaf fall.
- Responses to environmental stress.

The major phytohormones discussed in this chapter are:

1. Auxin.
2. Gibberellin.
3. Cytokinin.
4. Abscisic acid.
5. Ethylene.

---

# 5.2.2 AUXIN

Auxin is a phytohormone involved in plant growth and development.

A common natural auxin is indole-3-acetic acid, or IAA.

Auxin is produced mainly in:

- Shoot tips.
- Young leaves.
- Developing seeds and fruits.

### Functions of Auxin

Auxin:

1. Promotes cell elongation in shoots.
2. Controls phototropic responses.
3. Influences gravitropic responses.
4. Promotes apical dominance.
5. Stimulates root formation at suitable concentrations.
6. Can promote fruit development.
7. Can delay certain forms of leaf and fruit abscission.
8. Can be used in agriculture as a rooting agent.

---

# 5.2.3 AUXIN AND APICAL DOMINANCE

Apical dominance is the inhibition of lateral bud growth by the shoot apical bud.

The shoot tip produces auxin.

Auxin helps maintain dominance of the main shoot over lateral buds.

### If the shoot tip is removed

The source of auxin is reduced.

Lateral buds may then grow more actively.

This produces a bushier plant.

### Agricultural application

Pruning or removal of shoot tips can be used to encourage branching in certain crops.

---

# 5.2.4 AUXIN AND ROOT FORMATION

Auxin can stimulate root formation at suitable concentrations.

This is especially useful in vegetative propagation.

For example, a cutting from a plant can be treated with a suitable rooting hormone to encourage the formation of adventitious roots.

However, the effect of auxin depends on concentration and tissue type.

Very high auxin concentrations can inhibit root growth.

---

# 5.2.5 GIBBERELLIN

Gibberellins are phytohormones involved in growth and development.

They are produced in various actively growing tissues.

### Functions of Gibberellin

Gibberellins:

1. Promote stem elongation.
2. Promote growth of some leaves and fruits.
3. Break seed dormancy in some species.
4. Promote seed germination.
5. Stimulate enzyme production during seed germination.
6. Can promote flowering in some plants.
7. Can increase fruit size in some crops.

---

# 5.2.6 GIBBERELLIN AND SEED GERMINATION

Gibberellin plays an important role in the germination of many seeds.

When a seed absorbs water:

1. Metabolic activity increases.
2. Gibberellin production/signalling increases.
3. Gibberellin stimulates cells in the aleurone layer of cereal grains.
4. The aleurone cells produce hydrolytic enzymes.
5. One important enzyme is amylase.
6. Amylase breaks down starch stored in the endosperm.
7. Soluble sugars are released.
8. The growing embryo uses these sugars for respiration and growth.

### Simplified sequence

Water enters seed  
↓  
Gibberellin signalling increases  
↓  
Aleurone cells activated  
↓  
Amylase produced  
↓  
Starch hydrolysed  
↓  
Soluble sugars released  
↓  
Respiration and embryo growth

---

# 5.2.7 CYTOKININ

Cytokinins are phytohormones that promote cell division and influence plant development.

They are produced in various tissues, including actively growing roots.

### Functions of Cytokinin

Cytokinins:

1. Promote cell division.
2. Promote cell differentiation in combination with other hormones.
3. Stimulate lateral bud growth.
4. Can reduce apical dominance.
5. Delay leaf senescence.
6. Support nutrient mobilisation towards developing tissues.

### Cytokinin and Apical Dominance

Cytokinins generally promote lateral bud growth.

Auxin from the shoot tip promotes apical dominance.

The balance between auxin and cytokinin influences whether the main shoot or lateral shoots grow more strongly.

---

# 5.2.8 CYTOKININ AND SENESCENCE

Senescence is the process of ageing in plant organs.

Examples:

- Ageing leaves.
- Ageing flowers.
- Ageing fruits.

Cytokinins can delay leaf senescence.

This allows leaves to remain physiologically active for longer.

Cytokinins can help maintain:

- Protein synthesis.
- Chlorophyll.
- Cellular activity.

---

# 5.2.9 ABSCISIC ACID

Abscisic acid, commonly abbreviated as ABA, is a phytohormone involved especially in stress responses, stomatal regulation and seed dormancy.

### Functions of ABA

ABA:

1. Promotes stomatal closure during water stress.
2. Helps plants respond to drought.
3. Promotes seed dormancy.
4. Inhibits growth in certain situations.
5. Helps seeds tolerate unfavourable conditions.

---

# 5.2.10 ABA AND STOMATAL CLOSURE

When a plant experiences water stress:

1. Water availability decreases.
2. ABA concentration/signalling increases.
3. ABA acts on guard cells.
4. Ions are redistributed from guard cells.
5. Water leaves the guard cells by osmosis.
6. Guard cells become less turgid.
7. Stomatal pores close.
8. Water loss through transpiration decreases.

### Simplified sequence

Water stress  
↓  
ABA signalling increases  
↓  
Guard cell ion loss  
↓  
Water leaves guard cells  
↓  
Guard cells become flaccid  
↓  
Stomata close  
↓  
Transpiration decreases

This is an important adaptation to drought.

---

# 5.2.11 ETHYLENE

Ethylene is a gaseous phytohormone.

Unlike most plant hormones, ethylene is volatile and can diffuse through air spaces.

It is especially important in:

- Fruit ripening.
- Leaf abscission.
- Senescence.
- Responses to mechanical stress.

### Functions of Ethylene

Ethylene:

1. Promotes fruit ripening.
2. Promotes senescence.
3. Promotes leaf and fruit abscission.
4. Helps plants respond to mechanical stress.
5. Can influence root and shoot growth.

---

# 5.2.12 ETHYLENE AND FRUIT RIPENING

Ethylene promotes the ripening of many fruits.

During ripening:

- Cell walls may soften.
- Starch may be converted into sugars.
- Acids may change.
- Pigments may change.
- Aroma compounds may be produced.

These changes alter the:

- Texture.
- Taste.
- Colour.
- Smell.

Ethylene can stimulate further ethylene production in many climacteric fruits, producing a coordinated ripening response.

---

# 5.2.13 ETHYLENE AND ABSCISSION

Abscission is the natural separation of a plant organ such as:

- Leaf.
- Flower.
- Fruit.

Ethylene can promote the development of an abscission zone.

Cells in this region become weakened, allowing the organ to detach.

This is part of normal plant development.

---

# 5.2.14 COMPARISON OF PHYTOHORMONES

| Phytohormone | Major functions |
|---|---|
| Auxin | Cell elongation, phototropism, apical dominance, root formation |
| Gibberellin | Stem elongation, seed germination, dormancy breaking, fruit growth |
| Cytokinin | Cell division, lateral bud growth, delays senescence |
| Abscisic acid | Stomatal closure, seed dormancy, drought response |
| Ethylene | Fruit ripening, senescence, abscission |

---

# 5.2.15 HORMONE BALANCE

Plant responses are not normally controlled by one hormone acting independently.

Different hormones interact with each other.

The final response depends on:

- Hormone concentration.
- Tissue type.
- Developmental stage.
- Environmental conditions.
- Presence of other hormones.

For example:

### Auxin + Cytokinin

The balance between auxin and cytokinin influences:

- Root formation.
- Shoot formation.
- Cell division.
- Apical dominance.

### Auxin + Gibberellin

Both can influence aspects of plant growth, including stem elongation and development.

### ABA + Ethylene

Both can be involved in responses associated with stress and ageing, although their effects depend on the tissue and conditions.

**Important:**

A phytohormone does not always have one effect under every condition. Its effect depends on concentration, target tissue and interactions with other hormones.

---

# 5.3 APPLICATION OF PHYTOHORMONES IN AGRICULTURE

Phytohormones and synthetic plant growth regulators are widely used in agriculture and horticulture.

They can be used to control:

- Root formation.
- Plant height.
- Fruit development.
- Fruit ripening.
- Seed germination.
- Dormancy.
- Branching.
- Fruit drop.
- Tissue culture.

---

# 5.3.1 AUXIN IN AGRICULTURE

## A. Rooting of Cuttings

Auxin-based rooting hormones can stimulate the formation of adventitious roots.

### Application

A suitable auxin preparation can be applied to the base of a plant cutting before it is placed in a suitable growing medium.

### Benefit

- Increases the likelihood of root formation.
- Supports vegetative propagation.
- Allows desirable plants to be reproduced efficiently.

---

# 5.3.2 AUXIN AND FRUIT DEVELOPMENT

Auxin can promote fruit development.

In some crops, auxin treatments can stimulate fruit growth or fruit development under controlled conditions.

Certain synthetic auxins can also be used to reduce premature fruit drop in some crops.

The response depends on:

- Crop species.
- Variety.
- Concentration.
- Timing of application.

---

# 5.3.3 SYNTHETIC AUXINS AS HERBICIDES

Some synthetic auxin-like compounds are used as selective herbicides.

They interfere with normal growth regulation in susceptible broad-leaved weeds.

At sufficiently high doses, they cause abnormal growth and eventually plant death.

Some grasses are relatively less affected by certain selective auxin herbicides, allowing them to be used in particular crops.

**Important:**

The effectiveness and selectivity depend on:

- Herbicide.
- Concentration.
- Plant species.
- Growth stage.
- Application conditions.

---

# 5.3.4 GIBBERELLIN IN AGRICULTURE

Gibberellins have several agricultural applications.

## A. Increasing Stem Elongation

Gibberellin can stimulate stem elongation.

This can be useful in controlled horticultural applications.

## B. Breaking Seed Dormancy

Gibberellin can help overcome dormancy in certain seeds.

This can promote germination when suitable environmental conditions are present.

## C. Increasing Fruit Size

Gibberellin treatments can increase the size of certain fruits.

For example, gibberellins are used in some grape production systems.

## D. Improving Fruit Quality

Gibberellins can influence fruit development and may affect characteristics such as fruit size and shape in certain crops.

---

# 5.3.5 CYTOKININ IN AGRICULTURE

Cytokinins are particularly important in plant tissue culture.

### Tissue Culture

Plant tissue culture involves growing plant cells, tissues or organs under controlled conditions on a nutrient medium.

Plant hormones are used to regulate development.

The balance between auxin and cytokinin influences whether tissues develop:

- Roots.
- Shoots.
- Undifferentiated callus tissue.

### General principle

A relatively higher auxin influence often favours root formation.

A relatively higher cytokinin influence often favours shoot formation.

The exact response depends on plant species, tissue type and hormone concentrations.

---

# 5.3.6 CYTOKININ AND DELAYING SENESCENCE

Cytokinins can delay leaf ageing.

This may help:

- Maintain chlorophyll.
- Maintain photosynthetic activity.
- Extend the useful life of leaves.
- Maintain quality of certain harvested plant tissues.

This application can be useful in horticultural and post-harvest management.

---

# 5.3.7 ABA IN AGRICULTURE

Abscisic acid can be used or studied in agriculture because of its effects on:

- Stomatal closure.
- Seed dormancy.
- Stress responses.

Under water-limited conditions, ABA signalling helps plants reduce water loss by promoting stomatal closure.

However, excessive stomatal closure also reduces carbon dioxide entry and may reduce photosynthesis.

Therefore, plant growth and water conservation must be balanced.

---

# 5.3.8 ETHYLENE IN AGRICULTURE

Ethylene is particularly important in fruit production and post-harvest management.

## A. Fruit Ripening

Ethylene can be used to promote and coordinate ripening of certain fruits.

This can help farmers and distributors manage the timing of ripening.

## B. Controlled Ripening

Fruit may be harvested before complete ripening and then exposed to controlled ethylene conditions later.

This allows ripening to be coordinated during storage and distribution.

## C. Fruit Abscission

Ethylene can influence the separation of plant organs.

This can be useful in some agricultural practices where controlled fruit or leaf drop is desired.

---

# 5.3.9 APPLICATION OF PHYTOHORMONES IN TISSUE CULTURE

Plant tissue culture is an important agricultural application of plant hormones.

A small piece of plant tissue is placed on a sterile nutrient medium containing suitable concentrations of:

- Auxin.
- Cytokinin.
- Other nutrients and growth factors.

### General Process

1. A suitable plant tissue is selected.
2. The tissue is sterilised.
3. It is placed on a sterile nutrient medium.
4. Hormone concentrations are adjusted.
5. Cells divide and form tissue.
6. Shoots or roots develop.
7. Plantlets are transferred to growing conditions.
8. Plants are gradually adapted to normal environmental conditions.

### Importance

Tissue culture can produce:

- Large numbers of plants.
- Genetically similar plants from a selected parent.
- Disease-free planting material when appropriate clean starting material and techniques are used.
- Plants throughout the year under controlled conditions.

---

# 5.3.10 AUXIN-TO-CYTOKININ BALANCE IN TISSUE CULTURE

The relative concentration of auxin and cytokinin affects the developmental pathway of cultured plant tissues.

A simplified model is:

### Higher auxin relative to cytokinin

→ Root development is favoured.

### Higher cytokinin relative to auxin

→ Shoot development is favoured.

### Intermediate or suitable balance

→ Callus formation may occur under appropriate conditions.

**Important:**

This is a general principle rather than a universal fixed ratio. The exact response depends on plant species, tissue and culture conditions.

---

# 5.3.11 AGRICULTURAL IMPORTANCE OF PHYTOHORMONES

Phytohormones can help farmers control plant development more efficiently.

Applications include:

1. Promoting root formation.
2. Controlling plant height.
3. Promoting seed germination.
4. Breaking seed dormancy.
5. Increasing fruit size.
6. Controlling fruit ripening.
7. Delaying senescence.
8. Controlling fruit or leaf abscission.
9. Producing plants through tissue culture.
10. Managing certain weeds using synthetic hormone-like compounds.

---

# 5.3.12 ADVANTAGES OF USING PHYTOHORMONES

### 1. Improved crop production

Hormones can be used to influence growth and development.

### 2. Faster propagation

Auxin can encourage rooting of cuttings.

### 3. Controlled ripening

Ethylene can help coordinate fruit ripening.

### 4. Improved fruit development

Gibberellins and other regulators can influence fruit size and development in certain crops.

### 5. Mass production of plants

Auxin and cytokinin are important in tissue culture.

### 6. Improved agricultural management

Hormones allow farmers to manipulate certain aspects of plant growth under controlled conditions.

---

# 5.3.13 LIMITATIONS AND RISKS

Phytohormones must be used carefully.

### 1. Concentration matters

A hormone can have different effects at different concentrations.

### 2. Plant species differ

The same treatment may produce different responses in different species.

### 3. Timing matters

The developmental stage of the plant can affect the response.

### 4. Environmental conditions matter

Temperature, light, water availability and nutrient conditions can influence hormone responses.

### 5. Excessive application can damage plants

Too much of a growth regulator can cause abnormal growth or inhibit normal development.

### 6. Hormones interact

A treatment involving one hormone can affect the action of other hormones.

Therefore, agricultural use requires appropriate dosage and timing.

---

# IMPORTANT COMPARISONS

## 1. Positive vs Negative Tropism

Positive tropism:

- Growth towards the stimulus.

Negative tropism:

- Growth away from the stimulus.

Example:

- Shoot towards light = positive phototropism.
- Root away from light = negative phototropism.

---

## 2. Tropism vs Nastic Response

Tropism:

- Direction depends on stimulus direction.
- Usually involves directional growth.
- Example: phototropism.

Nastic response:

- Direction does not depend on stimulus direction.
- Often involves turgor changes.
- Example: Mimosa leaf folding.

---

## 3. Auxin vs Cytokinin

Auxin:

- Promotes shoot cell elongation.
- Promotes apical dominance.
- Can stimulate root formation at suitable concentrations.
- Important in phototropism.

Cytokinin:

- Promotes cell division.
- Promotes lateral bud growth.
- Delays senescence.
- Important in tissue culture.

---

## 4. Gibberellin vs Abscisic Acid

Gibberellin:

- Promotes growth.
- Promotes seed germination in many species.
- Can break dormancy.

ABA:

- Promotes seed dormancy.
- Promotes stomatal closure.
- Helps plants respond to drought.

Their effects can therefore oppose each other in processes such as seed dormancy and germination.

---

## 5. ABA vs Ethylene

ABA:

- Important in water-stress responses.
- Promotes stomatal closure.
- Promotes seed dormancy.

Ethylene:

- Promotes fruit ripening.
- Promotes senescence.
- Promotes abscission.

---

# COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Do not say phototropism is simply "movement towards light." It is a directional growth response to light.
2. Do not confuse tropism with nastic responses.
3. Do not say all auxin promotes root growth regardless of concentration.
4. Do not forget that high auxin concentrations can inhibit root elongation.
5. Do not state that cytokinin only promotes roots. Its major roles include cell division and lateral shoot growth.
6. Do not confuse gibberellin with ABA in seed dormancy.
7. Do not say ABA promotes stomatal opening during drought. ABA promotes stomatal closure.
8. Do not confuse ethylene with auxin.
9. Do not say all fruits respond to ethylene in exactly the same way.
10. Do not assume one fixed auxin-to-cytokinin ratio works for every plant tissue culture system.
11. Do not assume every phytohormone has one effect regardless of concentration and tissue.
12. Do not forget that plant hormones interact with one another.

# EXAM TIPS

## Question: Explain phototropism in a shoot.

Suggested answer:

1. Light is detected by the shoot.
2. Auxin becomes concentrated on the shaded side.
3. Auxin promotes cell elongation on the shaded side.
4. Cells on the shaded side elongate faster than cells on the illuminated side.
5. Unequal growth causes the shoot to bend towards the light.

## Question: Explain root gravitropism.

Suggested answer:

1. Gravity causes auxin to accumulate on the lower side of the root.
2. A relatively high concentration of auxin inhibits cell elongation in the root.
3. Cells on the upper side elongate faster.
4. Unequal growth causes the root to bend downwards.

## Question: State two functions of gibberellin.

Suggested answer:

1. Promotes stem elongation.
2. Promotes seed germination or breaks seed dormancy in some species.

## Question: Explain how ABA helps a plant survive drought.

Suggested answer:

1. Water stress increases ABA signalling.
2. ABA causes stomata to close.
3. Water loss through transpiration decreases.
4. This helps conserve water.

## Question: Explain the role of ethylene in fruit ripening.

Suggested answer:

1. Ethylene promotes biochemical changes associated with fruit ripening.
2. Starch may be converted into sugars.
3. Cell walls may soften.
4. Changes in pigments, acids and aroma compounds occur.
5. The fruit becomes ripe.

# SPM-STYLE PRACTICE QUESTIONS

## QUESTION 1

Define phototropism.

**Suggested answer:**

Phototropism is the directional growth response of a plant towards or away from light.

## QUESTION 2

Differentiate between positive and negative tropism.

**Suggested answer:**

Positive tropism is growth towards a stimulus, whereas negative tropism is growth away from a stimulus.

## QUESTION 3

Explain why a shoot bends towards light.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Light causes auxin to become concentrated on the shaded side of the shoot.
2. Auxin promotes cell elongation on the shaded side.
3. Cells on the shaded side elongate faster.
4. Unequal growth causes the shoot to bend towards the light.

## QUESTION 4

Explain how a horizontally placed root bends downwards.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Gravity causes auxin to accumulate on the lower side of the root.
2. A relatively high auxin concentration inhibits cell elongation in the root.
3. Cells on the upper side elongate faster.
4. Unequal growth causes the root to bend downwards.

## QUESTION 5

A student touches the leaves of Mimosa pudica and observes that the leaflets fold.

Identify the type of response.

**Suggested answer:**

The response is seismonasty.

## QUESTION 6

Explain why the response of Mimosa pudica is classified as a nastic response rather than a tropism.

**Suggested answer:**

The response does not depend on the direction from which the stimulus is applied. Therefore, it is a nastic response rather than a directional tropic growth response.

## QUESTION 7

State three functions of auxin.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Promotes cell elongation in shoots.
2. Controls phototropic responses.
3. Promotes apical dominance.

## QUESTION 8

Explain the role of gibberellin during seed germination in cereal grains.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Gibberellin signalling increases after the seed absorbs water.
2. It stimulates aleurone cells.
3. Aleurone cells produce hydrolytic enzymes such as amylase.
4. Amylase breaks down starch into soluble sugars.
5. The sugars are used by the embryo for respiration and growth.

## QUESTION 9

Explain how ABA helps a plant during drought.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Water stress increases ABA signalling.
2. ABA causes ions to leave guard cells.
3. Water leaves the guard cells by osmosis.
4. Guard cells become less turgid.
5. Stomata close.
6. Water loss through transpiration decreases.

## QUESTION 10

State two functions of ethylene.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Promotes fruit ripening.
2. Promotes senescence or abscission.

## QUESTION 11

Explain how phytohormones are used in tissue culture.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Plant tissues are grown on a nutrient medium.
2. Auxin and cytokinin are added in suitable concentrations.
3. The relative balance of the hormones influences tissue development.
4. A higher relative auxin influence can favour root formation.
5. A higher relative cytokinin influence can favour shoot formation.

## QUESTION 12

Explain one agricultural application of auxin.

**Suggested answer:**

Auxin can be applied to plant cuttings to promote the formation of adventitious roots. This improves the success of vegetative propagation.

## QUESTION 13

Explain two agricultural applications of gibberellin.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Gibberellin can be used to promote seed germination or break dormancy in suitable seeds.
2. Gibberellin can be used to increase the size of certain fruits, such as grapes.

## QUESTION 14

Compare auxin and cytokinin in plant development.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Auxin promotes shoot cell elongation, whereas cytokinin promotes cell division.
2. Auxin promotes apical dominance, whereas cytokinin promotes lateral bud growth.
3. Auxin can promote root formation at suitable concentrations, whereas cytokinin can favour shoot formation in suitable tissue culture conditions.

## QUESTION 15

Explain why phytohormones must be applied at appropriate concentrations in agriculture.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Hormone effects depend on concentration.
2. Different concentrations can produce different responses.
3. Excessive concentrations may inhibit growth or cause abnormal development.
4. The response also depends on plant species and tissue type.
5. Therefore, the appropriate concentration must be selected for the intended agricultural application.

# CHAPTER 5 — FINAL REVISION SUMMARY

## 5.1 TYPES OF RESPONSES

- Plants respond to environmental stimuli.
- Tropism is a directional growth response.
- Positive tropism is growth towards a stimulus.
- Negative tropism is growth away from a stimulus.
- Phototropism responds to light.
- Geotropism/gravitropism responds to gravity.
- Hydrotropism responds to water.
- Thigmotropism responds to touch.
- Chemotropism responds to chemicals.
- Nastic responses do not depend on the direction of the stimulus.
- Seismonasty is a response to mechanical stimulation.
- Nyctinasty is associated with daily light/dark cycles.

## 5.2 PHYTOHORMONES

### Auxin

- Cell elongation.
- Phototropism.
- Apical dominance.
- Root formation at suitable concentrations.

### Gibberellin

- Stem elongation.
- Seed germination.
- Breaking dormancy.
- Fruit growth.

### Cytokinin

- Cell division.
- Lateral bud growth.
- Delays senescence.
- Important in tissue culture.

### Abscisic acid

- Stomatal closure.
- Drought response.
- Seed dormancy.

### Ethylene

- Fruit ripening.
- Senescence.
- Abscission.

## 5.3 AGRICULTURAL APPLICATIONS

- Auxin → rooting of cuttings.
- Auxin → fruit development and some fruit-drop control.
- Synthetic auxin-like compounds → selective weed control.
- Gibberellin → seed germination/dormancy breaking.
- Gibberellin → fruit growth.
- Cytokinin → tissue culture and delayed senescence.
- ABA → water-stress management and dormancy-related applications.
- Ethylene → controlled fruit ripening.
- Auxin + cytokinin → regulation of root/shoot development in tissue culture.
