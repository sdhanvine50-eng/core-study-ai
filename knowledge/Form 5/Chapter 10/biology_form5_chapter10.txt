# 10.1 THREATS TO THE ENVIRONMENT

## 10.1.1 Introduction to Environmental Sustainability

Environmental sustainability refers to the responsible use and management of natural resources to meet present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

The environment provides essential resources such as:

- Clean air.
- Clean water.
- Food.
- Fertile soil.
- Forest resources.
- Minerals.
- Energy resources.
- Habitats for organisms.

Human activities can damage ecosystems when resources are used excessively or waste and pollutants are released into the environment.

Environmental sustainability aims to:

1. Protect biodiversity.
2. Maintain ecological balance.
3. Reduce pollution.
4. Conserve natural resources.
5. Reduce environmental damage.
6. Ensure resources remain available for future generations.

## 10.1.2 Human Activities That Threaten the Environment

### A. DEFORESTATION

Deforestation is the large-scale removal of trees and forests, usually to make land available for other purposes.

**Causes:**

- Clearing land for agriculture.
- Logging for timber.
- Urban development.
- Construction of roads and infrastructure.
- Mining activities.
- Establishment of plantations.

**Effects on the environment:**

1. **Habitat destruction**

   Forest organisms lose their natural habitats, reducing available food, shelter and breeding sites.

2. **Loss of biodiversity**

   Species may experience population declines or become locally extinct when their habitats are destroyed.

3. **Soil erosion**

   Tree roots help hold soil particles together. Removing trees exposes soil to rainfall and surface runoff.

4. **Increased atmospheric carbon dioxide**

   Fewer trees are available to absorb carbon dioxide through photosynthesis. Burning and decomposition of cleared vegetation can also release carbon dioxide.

5. **Disruption of the water cycle**

   Reduced transpiration can affect local atmospheric moisture and rainfall patterns.

6. **Increased risk of flooding**

   Reduced vegetation and changes in soil conditions can increase surface runoff.

**Possible solutions:**

- Practise sustainable logging.
- Replant trees through reforestation.
- Protect existing forests.
- Establish protected forest reserves.
- Reduce unnecessary use of paper and timber.
- Improve agricultural land management.

### B. AIR POLLUTION

Air pollution occurs when harmful substances enter the atmosphere at concentrations that can negatively affect living organisms and the environment.

**Sources of air pollutants:**

- Vehicle exhaust emissions.
- Industrial activities.
- Burning of fossil fuels.
- Open burning.
- Forest fires.
- Construction activities.

**Important air pollutants:**

| Pollutant | Main sources | Possible effects |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon monoxide (CO) | Incomplete combustion | Reduces oxygen transport in blood |
| Sulphur dioxide (SO₂) | Burning sulphur-containing fuels | Respiratory irritation and acid rain |
| Nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) | Vehicles and power stations | Smog and acid rain |
| Particulate matter | Smoke, dust and combustion | Respiratory and cardiovascular problems |
| Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | Fossil fuel combustion and deforestation | Enhances the greenhouse effect |
| Ground-level ozone (O₃) | Reactions involving NOₓ and volatile organic compounds in sunlight | Damages respiratory tissues and plants |

**Effects of air pollution:**

1. Causes respiratory problems.
2. Reduces visibility through haze.
3. Damages plant tissues.
4. Contributes to acid rain.
5. Contributes to climate change through greenhouse gas emissions.
6. Can damage buildings and monuments.

### C. WATER POLLUTION

Water pollution occurs when harmful substances enter rivers, lakes, seas or groundwater and reduce water quality.

**Sources:**

- Untreated sewage.
- Industrial waste.
- Agricultural fertilisers.
- Pesticides.
- Oil spills.
- Plastic waste.
- Detergents.
- Soil and sediment runoff.

**Effects of water pollution:**

1. **Death of aquatic organisms**

   Toxic chemicals and low dissolved oxygen can cause aquatic organisms to die.

2. **Eutrophication**

   Excessive nutrients, especially nitrates and phosphates, can cause rapid growth of algae and aquatic plants.

3. **Bioaccumulation**

   Certain pollutants accumulate in an organism when their rate of absorption exceeds their rate of elimination.

4. **Biomagnification**

   Concentrations of certain persistent pollutants increase at higher trophic levels in a food chain.

5. **Spread of waterborne diseases**

   Water contaminated with disease-causing microorganisms can transmit infections.

6. **Reduction in water quality**

   Polluted water becomes unsuitable for drinking, recreation and some industrial or agricultural uses.

### D. SOIL POLLUTION

Soil pollution occurs when harmful chemicals, waste or other contaminants accumulate in soil.

**Sources:**

- Excessive use of pesticides.
- Excessive use of chemical fertilisers.
- Improper disposal of industrial waste.
- Landfills that are poorly managed.
- Mining activities.
- Oil and chemical spills.

**Effects:**

1. Reduces soil quality and fertility.
2. Harms soil microorganisms.
3. Contaminates crops.
4. Pollutants may enter groundwater.
5. Toxic substances may enter food chains.
6. Can reduce plant growth.

**Solutions:**

- Apply fertilisers responsibly.
- Use integrated pest management.
- Treat industrial waste before disposal.
- Manage landfills properly.
- Restore contaminated land where possible.

## 10.1.3 Greenhouse Effect and Global Warming

### A. Greenhouse Effect

The greenhouse effect is a natural process in which greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, helping retain heat in Earth's atmosphere.

This natural process keeps Earth warm enough to support life.

**Main greenhouse gases:**

- Carbon dioxide (CO₂).
- Methane (CH₄).
- Nitrous oxide (N₂O).
- Water vapour.
- Ozone.
- Certain industrial gases.

**How the greenhouse effect occurs:**

1. Solar radiation enters Earth's atmosphere.
2. Some radiation is reflected back into space.
3. Earth's surface absorbs some of the incoming energy and warms up.
4. The warmed surface emits infrared radiation.
5. Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit some of this infrared radiation.
6. Some energy is directed back towards Earth's surface and lower atmosphere.

Human activities increase the concentration of certain greenhouse gases, strengthening the greenhouse effect.

### B. Global Warming

Global warming refers to the long-term increase in Earth's average surface temperature, mainly due to the increased concentration of greenhouse gases from human activities.

**Human activities that contribute to global warming:**

- Burning fossil fuels.
- Deforestation.
- Industrial processes.
- Agriculture and livestock farming.
- Waste decomposition in landfills.

**Effects of global warming:**

1. Rising average temperatures.
2. Melting glaciers and ice sheets.
3. Rising sea levels.
4. Changes in rainfall patterns.
5. More frequent or intense heat extremes.
6. Increased stress on ecosystems.
7. Changes in species distribution.
8. Increased risks to agriculture and food security.

**Ways to reduce global warming:**

- Reduce fossil fuel consumption.
- Use renewable energy.
- Improve energy efficiency.
- Protect forests.
- Reforest degraded areas.
- Reduce methane emissions.
- Use public transportation.
- Reduce unnecessary waste.

**IMPORTANT COMPARISON:**

- Greenhouse effect: The process by which greenhouse gases help retain heat in the atmosphere.
- Enhanced greenhouse effect: Additional heat retention caused by increased greenhouse gas concentrations.
- Global warming: The long-term rise in Earth's average temperature.

The natural greenhouse effect is necessary for life. The problem is its human-driven enhancement.

## 10.1.4 Acid Rain

Acid rain refers to precipitation that is more acidic than normal due mainly to sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides reacting in the atmosphere to form acids.

**Main causes:**

- Burning fossil fuels in power stations.
- Industrial emissions.
- Vehicle emissions.
- Certain natural sources, such as volcanic activity.

**Formation of acid rain:**

1. Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides are released into the atmosphere.
2. These gases undergo chemical reactions involving oxygen and water.
3. Sulphuric acid and nitric acid can form.
4. The acids enter precipitation and fall to Earth's surface.

**Effects of acid rain:**

1. Increases the acidity of lakes and rivers.
2. Can harm fish and other aquatic organisms.
3. Leaches essential mineral nutrients from soil.
4. Can release toxic aluminium ions into soil and water.
5. Damages leaves and reduces plant growth.
6. Corrodes buildings and structures made from certain materials.

**Prevention:**

- Reduce sulphur dioxide emissions.
- Reduce nitrogen oxide emissions.
- Use cleaner energy sources.
- Install suitable emission-control systems.
- Improve energy efficiency.
- Reduce dependence on fossil fuels.

## 10.1.5 Eutrophication

Eutrophication is the enrichment of a body of water with nutrients, especially nitrates and phosphates, which can cause excessive growth of algae and aquatic plants.

Excessive nutrient input can lead to cultural eutrophication, which is accelerated by human activities.

**Sources of excess nutrients:**

- Fertiliser runoff from farms.
- Untreated sewage.
- Detergents containing phosphates.
- Animal waste.
- Certain industrial discharges.

**Process of eutrophication:**

1. Excess nitrates and phosphates enter a water body.
2. Algae reproduce rapidly, forming an algal bloom.
3. The algal bloom reduces light penetration into deeper water.
4. Submerged aquatic plants receive less light and photosynthesise less.
5. Some aquatic plants die.
6. Decomposers break down dead organisms.
7. Decomposers use dissolved oxygen during aerobic respiration.
8. Dissolved oxygen levels decrease.
9. Fish and other aerobic aquatic organisms may die due to oxygen shortage.

**Effects:**

- Reduced dissolved oxygen.
- Fish deaths.
- Loss of aquatic biodiversity.
- Unpleasant smell.
- Reduced water quality.
- Disruption of aquatic food webs.

**Prevention:**

- Treat sewage before releasing it.
- Control fertiliser application.
- Prevent agricultural runoff.
- Use phosphate-free detergents where suitable.
- Maintain vegetation buffer zones near waterways.

**IMPORTANT EXAM POINT:**

The main reason fish die during severe eutrophication is often the reduction in dissolved oxygen caused by the respiration of decomposers breaking down dead organic matter.

## 10.1.6 Bioaccumulation and Biomagnification

### A. Bioaccumulation

Bioaccumulation is the gradual accumulation of a substance, such as a persistent pollutant, inside an organism over time.

It occurs when the rate of absorption is greater than the rate of elimination.

Examples of substances that may bioaccumulate include:

- Mercury.
- Certain persistent pesticides.
- Some industrial pollutants.

### B. Biomagnification

Biomagnification is the increase in concentration of certain persistent pollutants at successive trophic levels in a food chain.

**Illustrative food chain:**

Water → Plankton → Small fish → Large fish → Fish-eating bird

A persistent pollutant may become more concentrated in organisms at higher trophic levels because predators consume many contaminated prey.

**Differences:**

| Bioaccumulation | Biomagnification |
|---|---|
| Occurs within an organism over time | Occurs across trophic levels |
| Pollutant builds up in an organism | Pollutant concentration increases higher in a food chain |
| Depends on absorption and elimination | Depends on transfer through feeding relationships |

**IMPORTANT:**

Not every pollutant biomagnifies. Biomagnification is most associated with substances that persist in the environment and accumulate in organisms.

## 10.1.7 Loss of Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms at genetic, species and ecosystem levels.

**Threats to biodiversity:**

- Habitat destruction.
- Habitat fragmentation.
- Pollution.
- Climate change.
- Overexploitation.
- Invasive alien species.
- Illegal wildlife trade.

**Consequences of biodiversity loss:**

1. Disruption of food webs.
2. Loss of genetic resources.
3. Reduced ecosystem stability.
4. Loss of potential medicinal resources.
5. Reduced ecosystem services such as pollination.
6. Increased vulnerability to environmental changes.

# 10.2 PRESERVATION, CONSERVATION AND RESTORATION OF ECOSYSTEMS

## 10.2.1 Meaning of Preservation

Preservation refers to protecting natural environments and organisms from harmful human interference.

Its main aim is to maintain ecosystems in their existing condition.

**Examples:**

- Establishing protected areas.
- Preventing illegal logging.
- Protecting endangered species.
- Preventing destruction of natural habitats.
- Restricting damaging human activities in sensitive ecosystems.

Preservation is especially important for ecosystems that are rare, fragile or highly valuable.

## 10.2.2 Meaning of Conservation

Conservation refers to the responsible management and sustainable use of natural resources to prevent depletion and maintain biodiversity.

Unlike strict protection, conservation can allow controlled human use of resources.

**Examples:**

- Sustainable forestry.
- Controlled fishing.
- Wildlife management.
- Responsible water use.
- Replanting trees.
- Protecting breeding populations.
- Sustainable agriculture.

**Importance of conservation:**

1. Maintains biodiversity.
2. Prevents extinction.
3. Protects habitats.
4. Maintains ecological balance.
5. Preserves natural resources.
6. Supports future generations.
7. Protects ecosystem services.

## 10.2.3 Meaning of Restoration

Ecological restoration is the process of assisting the recovery of an ecosystem that has been degraded, damaged or destroyed.

The aim is to improve ecosystem structure, function and biodiversity.

**Examples:**

- Replanting native trees in degraded forests.
- Restoring mangrove ecosystems.
- Rehabilitating damaged riverbanks.
- Restoring wetlands.
- Removing invasive alien species where appropriate.
- Rehabilitating mined land.

**Steps involved in ecosystem restoration:**

1. Identify the cause and extent of environmental damage.
2. Assess the condition of the ecosystem.
3. Remove or reduce the main sources of damage.
4. Reintroduce suitable native vegetation where appropriate.
5. Restore habitats and natural processes.
6. Monitor the recovery of the ecosystem.
7. Continue management when necessary.

Restoration may take many years, and a restored ecosystem may not become exactly identical to its original condition.

## 10.2.4 Differences Between Preservation, Conservation and Restoration

| Preservation | Conservation | Restoration |
|---|---|---|
| Protects ecosystems from harmful interference | Manages resources responsibly | Helps damaged ecosystems recover |
| Focuses on maintaining existing conditions | Focuses on sustainable use and protection | Focuses on repairing ecological damage |
| Example: Protecting an untouched forest | Example: Sustainable timber harvesting | Example: Replanting a degraded forest |

**EXAM TIP:**

If a question asks for the difference:

- Preservation = protect.
- Conservation = manage sustainably.
- Restoration = repair and recover.

## 10.2.5 In Situ Conservation

In situ conservation refers to conserving organisms within their natural habitats.

**Examples:**

- National parks.
- Wildlife sanctuaries.
- Marine protected areas.
- Forest reserves.
- Nature reserves.

**Advantages:**

1. Protects organisms in their natural habitats.
2. Conserves many species together.
3. Maintains natural ecological interactions.
4. Allows natural breeding and evolution.
5. Protects habitats and ecosystems.

**Limitations:**

1. Natural disasters can damage protected habitats.
2. Poaching may still occur.
3. Protected areas require continuous monitoring.
4. Some species may remain threatened by disease or climate change.

## 10.2.6 Ex Situ Conservation

Ex situ conservation refers to conserving organisms outside their natural habitats under controlled conditions.

**Examples:**

- Zoos.
- Botanical gardens.
- Seed banks.
- Gene banks.
- Captive breeding programmes.
- Aquariums.
- Tissue culture collections.

**Advantages:**

1. Provides protection from some threats in natural habitats.
2. Allows controlled breeding.
3. Supports scientific research.
4. Can help maintain endangered species.
5. Seeds and genetic material can be stored for future use.

**Limitations:**

1. Can be expensive.
2. Limited space may restrict the number of organisms kept.
3. Captive organisms may lose certain natural behaviours.
4. Genetic diversity may be limited if breeding populations are small.
5. Reintroducing organisms into the wild can be difficult.

## 10.2.7 Comparison Between In Situ and Ex Situ Conservation

| In situ | Ex situ |
|---|---|
| Conservation in natural habitats | Conservation outside natural habitats |
| Protects ecosystems and natural interactions | Protects selected organisms or genetic material |
| Examples: national parks and wildlife reserves | Examples: zoos and seed banks |
| Allows natural ecological processes | Uses controlled conditions |
| Habitat protection is essential | Requires suitable facilities and management |

**IMPORTANT:**

In situ and ex situ conservation are complementary approaches. Both may be needed to protect threatened species effectively.

## 10.2.8 Sustainable Management of Natural Resources

Natural resources should be managed so that their use does not exceed their ability to recover or be replenished.

**Examples:**

1. **Forests**

   - Use selective logging.
   - Replant trees.
   - Protect high-conservation-value forests.
   - Reduce illegal logging.

2. **Fisheries**

   - Set suitable fishing limits.
   - Protect breeding seasons.
   - Reduce destructive fishing methods.
   - Establish marine protected areas.

3. **Water**

   - Prevent water pollution.
   - Treat wastewater.
   - Reduce unnecessary water consumption.
   - Protect water catchment areas.

4. **Soil**

   - Practise crop rotation.
   - Maintain vegetation cover.
   - Reduce excessive pesticide use.
   - Apply suitable erosion-control methods.

5. **Wildlife**

   - Protect breeding habitats.
   - Prevent illegal hunting.
   - Maintain wildlife corridors.
   - Support responsible conservation programmes.

# 10.3 PRACTICES IN ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY

## 10.3.1 Introduction

Environmental sustainability can be improved through responsible practices by individuals, communities, industries and governments.

These practices aim to reduce resource consumption, prevent pollution and minimise environmental damage.

One important approach is the 5R concept.

## 10.3.2 The 5R Concept

### A. REFUSE

Refuse means avoiding products or materials that are unnecessary or harmful to the environment.

**Examples:**

- Refuse unnecessary single-use plastic items.
- Avoid products with excessive packaging.
- Choose reusable alternatives.
- Avoid disposable items when a suitable alternative exists.

**Environmental benefits:**

- Reduces waste generation.
- Reduces demand for raw materials.
- Reduces unnecessary packaging.
- Prevents some plastic waste from entering ecosystems.

### B. REDUCE

Reduce means using fewer resources and producing less waste.

**Examples:**

- Switch off unused electrical appliances.
- Use water responsibly.
- Buy only what is needed.
- Reduce food waste.
- Use digital documents when appropriate.

**Benefits:**

- Conserves natural resources.
- Reduces energy consumption.
- Reduces waste disposal.
- Reduces greenhouse gas emissions associated with production and transport.

### C. REUSE

Reuse means using an item again instead of discarding it after one use.

**Examples:**

- Use reusable water bottles.
- Reuse suitable containers.
- Donate clothes and books.
- Repair damaged items.
- Use reusable shopping bags.

**Benefits:**

- Extends product lifespan.
- Reduces waste.
- Reduces demand for new products.
- Saves resources and energy used in manufacturing.

### D. RECYCLE

Recycle means processing waste materials into new products or raw materials.

**Examples:**

- Recycling paper.
- Recycling glass.
- Recycling aluminium cans.
- Recycling suitable plastics.
- Recycling certain electronic waste through authorised facilities.

**Benefits:**

- Reduces waste sent to landfills.
- Conserves raw materials.
- Can reduce energy consumption compared with producing certain materials from virgin resources.
- Reduces environmental pollution.

**IMPORTANT:**

Different materials require different recycling processes. Not every type of plastic or waste can be recycled through every local recycling programme.

### E. RECOVER

Recover means obtaining useful materials or energy from waste that cannot reasonably be reused or recycled.

**Examples:**

- Recovering energy from suitable waste through controlled waste-to-energy systems.
- Recovering metals from certain industrial waste.
- Recovering useful materials from electronic waste.

**Benefits:**

- Reduces the amount of waste requiring final disposal.
- Allows useful energy or materials to be obtained.
- Can reduce dependence on some new raw materials.

**IMPORTANT:**

Recovery must be properly managed because poorly controlled waste treatment can release harmful pollutants.

## 10.3.3 Comparison of the 5R Concept

| Practice | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Refuse | Avoid unnecessary products | Refuse unnecessary plastic bags |
| Reduce | Use fewer resources | Save electricity |
| Reuse | Use an item again | Reuse containers |
| Recycle | Process waste into new materials | Recycle aluminium cans |
| Recover | Obtain useful energy or materials from waste | Recover energy from suitable waste |

## 10.3.4 Sustainable Agriculture

Sustainable agriculture refers to farming practices that produce food while protecting natural resources and maintaining environmental quality over the long term.

**Practices:**

1. **Crop rotation**

   Different crops are grown in sequence on the same land.

   Benefits:
   - Helps maintain soil fertility.
   - Can interrupt pest and disease cycles.
   - Reduces dependence on continuous cultivation of one crop.

2. **Organic fertilisers**

   Examples include compost and animal manure.

   Benefits:
   - Add organic matter to soil.
   - Improve soil structure.
   - Support soil organisms.

3. **Integrated pest management (IPM)**

   Combines biological, cultural, physical and carefully selected chemical methods to control pests.

   Benefits:
   - Reduces unnecessary pesticide use.
   - Helps protect beneficial organisms.
   - Reduces environmental contamination.

4. **Contour farming**

   Crops are planted along the contours of sloping land.

   Benefits:
   - Slows surface runoff.
   - Reduces soil erosion.

5. **Terracing**

   Sloping land is converted into a series of steps.

   Benefits:
   - Reduces the speed of water runoff.
   - Helps reduce soil erosion.

6. **Agroforestry**

   Trees are grown together with crops or livestock.

   Benefits:
   - Provides additional habitats.
   - Can reduce soil erosion.
   - May improve carbon storage.
   - Provides additional products such as fruit or timber.

## 10.3.5 Sustainable Forestry

Sustainable forestry involves managing forests so that timber and other forest resources can be used without destroying the long-term health of forest ecosystems.

**Practices:**

- Selective logging.
- Reforestation.
- Reduced-impact logging.
- Protection of wildlife habitats.
- Prevention of illegal logging.
- Maintenance of forest corridors.
- Monitoring forest recovery.

**Selective logging:**

Only selected trees are harvested instead of clearing the entire forest.

**Advantages:**

1. Reduces large-scale habitat destruction.
2. Maintains more forest cover.
3. Can reduce soil erosion.
4. Allows some forest structure and biodiversity to remain.

However, selective logging still requires proper planning because poorly managed harvesting can damage surrounding trees, soil and habitats.

## 10.3.6 Sustainable Fisheries

Sustainable fisheries aim to maintain fish populations and aquatic ecosystems while allowing fishing activities to continue.

**Practices:**

- Follow fishing quotas.
- Protect breeding seasons.
- Avoid catching undersized fish.
- Use selective fishing equipment.
- Prevent destructive fishing methods.
- Establish marine protected areas.
- Monitor fish populations.

**Importance:**

1. Prevents overfishing.
2. Allows fish populations to reproduce.
3. Maintains aquatic food webs.
4. Protects marine biodiversity.
5. Supports long-term food security.

## 10.3.7 Sustainable Transportation

Transportation contributes to air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions, particularly when vehicles use fossil fuels.

**Sustainable practices:**

- Use public transportation.
- Walk or cycle for suitable short journeys.
- Carpool.
- Use fuel-efficient vehicles.
- Support suitable electric transport systems.
- Maintain vehicles properly.

**Benefits:**

- Reduces fuel consumption.
- Can reduce traffic congestion.
- Reduces emissions per passenger when public transport is used efficiently.
- Improves urban air quality.

## 10.3.8 Individual and Community Responsibilities

Individuals can contribute to environmental sustainability through daily actions.

**At home:**

- Separate recyclable waste.
- Reduce water consumption.
- Switch off unused electrical appliances.
- Avoid food waste.
- Use reusable products.

**At school:**

- Organise recycling programmes.
- Reduce paper waste.
- Maintain school gardens.
- Conduct environmental awareness campaigns.
- Keep drains and surroundings free from litter.

**In the community:**

- Participate in tree-planting programmes.
- Join river or beach clean-ups.
- Support conservation projects.
- Report illegal dumping to relevant authorities.
- Encourage responsible resource use.

**IMPORTANT:**

Environmental sustainability requires cooperation among individuals, communities, businesses and governments. Individual actions are valuable, but large-scale environmental problems also require effective policies, infrastructure and industry changes.

# 10.4 GREEN TECHNOLOGY

## 10.4.1 Meaning of Green Technology

Green technology refers to the development and application of products, equipment and systems that reduce negative environmental impacts and promote sustainable resource use.

Green technology aims to:

1. Reduce pollution.
2. Reduce greenhouse gas emissions.
3. Improve energy efficiency.
4. Conserve natural resources.
5. Reduce waste.
6. Promote renewable energy.
7. Support sustainable development.

Green technology is used in transportation, energy production, buildings, agriculture, waste management and water treatment.

## 10.4.2 Renewable Energy

Renewable energy comes from natural sources that are replenished continuously or over relatively short periods.

Examples include:

- Solar energy.
- Wind energy.
- Hydroelectric energy.
- Geothermal energy.
- Biomass energy.

### A. SOLAR ENERGY

Solar energy is energy obtained from sunlight.

Solar panels containing photovoltaic cells convert light energy directly into electrical energy.

**How photovoltaic cells work:**

1. Sunlight reaches the photovoltaic cell.
2. Light energy is absorbed by semiconductor material.
3. The absorbed energy releases charge carriers.
4. An electric current is produced through the photovoltaic system.
5. The electrical energy can be used directly, stored in batteries or supplied to an electrical grid.

**Advantages:**

- Renewable energy source.
- Produces electricity without direct combustion.
- Reduces dependence on fossil fuels.
- Can be installed on rooftops.
- Has relatively low operating emissions.

**Limitations:**

- Electricity production depends on sunlight.
- Initial installation can be expensive.
- Energy storage may be needed.
- Manufacturing and disposal of panels have environmental impacts.
- Large installations require land.

### B. WIND ENERGY

Wind energy is obtained from moving air.

Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy of wind into electrical energy.

**How a wind turbine works:**

1. Wind moves the turbine blades.
2. The blades rotate a rotor.
3. The rotor drives a generator directly or through a gearbox.
4. The generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.

**Advantages:**

- Renewable.
- Produces electricity without direct fuel combustion.
- Reduces dependence on fossil fuels.
- Has low operating greenhouse gas emissions.

**Limitations:**

- Depends on wind availability.
- Suitable locations may be limited.
- Can cause noise and visual impacts.
- May affect birds and bats if poorly located.
- Requires suitable transmission infrastructure.

### C. HYDROELECTRIC ENERGY

Hydroelectric energy is generated using the energy of moving or falling water.

**How hydroelectric power generation works:**

1. Water stored at a higher level flows through a channel or pipe.
2. Moving water turns a turbine.
3. The turbine drives a generator.
4. The generator produces electricity.

**Advantages:**

- Renewable when water resources are managed sustainably.
- Can produce large amounts of electricity.
- Electricity generation can be controlled by managing water flow.
- Reservoirs may provide additional water-management benefits.

**Limitations:**

- Dams can flood large areas.
- Habitats may be destroyed.
- Fish migration may be disrupted.
- Communities may need to be relocated.
- Electricity production may be affected by drought.

### D. GEOTHERMAL ENERGY

Geothermal energy is heat obtained from within Earth.

The heat can be used directly or to generate electricity.

**How geothermal electricity generation works:**

1. Heat from underground warms water.
2. Hot water or steam is brought to the surface.
3. Steam or another working fluid drives a turbine.
4. The turbine turns a generator.
5. Electricity is produced.

**Advantages:**

- Provides a relatively reliable energy source in suitable locations.
- Has low operating greenhouse gas emissions.
- Requires less land than some large energy installations.

**Limitations:**

- Suitable locations are geographically limited.
- Drilling and construction can be expensive.
- Some systems may release underground gases.
- Poorly managed extraction can affect underground reservoirs.

### E. BIOMASS ENERGY

Biomass energy is obtained from organic materials such as plant matter and certain forms of organic waste.

Examples:

- Wood residues.
- Agricultural waste.
- Food-processing waste.
- Biogas from organic waste.

**Advantages:**

- Can use certain agricultural and organic wastes.
- Can reduce dependence on fossil fuels.
- Biogas can be used for heat and electricity.
- Some biomass systems can provide controllable energy.

**Limitations:**

- Burning biomass can release air pollutants.
- Unsustainable harvesting can cause deforestation.
- Large-scale energy crops may compete with food production.
- Biomass is not automatically carbon-neutral; its total environmental impact depends on how it is produced, transported and used.

## 10.4.3 Comparison of Renewable Energy Sources

| Energy source | Main energy input | Main technology | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solar | Sunlight | Photovoltaic panels | Depends on sunlight |
| Wind | Moving air | Wind turbines | Depends on wind |
| Hydroelectric | Moving or falling water | Turbines and generators | Can affect river ecosystems |
| Geothermal | Heat inside Earth | Steam or heat systems | Location-dependent |
| Biomass | Organic matter | Combustion or biogas systems | Can cause pollution if poorly managed |

## 10.4.4 Green Buildings

Green buildings are designed, constructed and operated to reduce environmental impacts throughout their life cycle.

**Features:**

1. Energy-efficient lighting.
2. Natural ventilation.
3. Suitable insulation.
4. Solar panels.
5. Rainwater harvesting.
6. Water-efficient fittings.
7. Sustainable building materials.
8. Green roofs and vegetation.
9. Efficient waste management systems.

**Advantages:**

- Reduces electricity consumption.
- Reduces water wastage.
- Improves indoor environmental quality.
- Reduces operating costs over time.
- Can reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

**Example:**

A building with natural lighting, efficient air-conditioning, rooftop solar panels and rainwater collection can reduce its dependence on external energy and water supplies.

## 10.4.5 Green Transportation

Green transportation aims to reduce the environmental impacts of moving people and goods.

**Examples:**

- Electric vehicles.
- Hybrid vehicles.
- Electric buses.
- Trains powered by low-carbon electricity.
- Cycling infrastructure.
- Walking-friendly urban planning.

### Electric Vehicles (EVs)

Electric vehicles use electric motors powered by electricity stored in rechargeable batteries.

**Advantages:**

1. Produce no exhaust emissions during driving.
2. Can reduce urban air pollution.
3. Are generally more energy-efficient than conventional petrol vehicles.
4. Can reduce greenhouse gas emissions when charged using low-carbon electricity.

**Limitations:**

1. Battery production requires raw materials.
2. Battery recycling and disposal must be properly managed.
3. Charging infrastructure may be limited.
4. Total emissions depend partly on how electricity is generated.

**IMPORTANT:**

Electric vehicles have no tailpipe emissions, but they are not completely emission-free across their entire life cycle.

## 10.4.6 Waste Management Technology

Green technology can improve the management and treatment of waste.

### A. RECYCLING TECHNOLOGY

Recycling technology processes used materials into materials that can be used again.

Examples:

- Paper recycling systems.
- Glass recycling.
- Metal recovery.
- Plastic sorting and processing.

**Benefits:**

- Reduces waste sent to landfills.
- Conserves raw materials.
- Can reduce energy consumption.
- Reduces some forms of pollution.

### B. COMPOSTING

Composting is the biological decomposition of suitable organic waste by microorganisms under managed conditions.

Examples of compostable materials:

- Fruit and vegetable scraps.
- Dry leaves.
- Grass clippings.
- Certain food-processing waste.

**Benefits:**

- Reduces organic waste sent to landfills.
- Produces compost that can improve soil.
- Recycles nutrients.
- Can reduce methane emissions when compared with uncontrolled anaerobic decomposition in landfills.

### C. BIOGAS TECHNOLOGY

Biogas is produced when microorganisms break down organic matter under anaerobic conditions, meaning in the absence of oxygen.

Biogas commonly contains methane and carbon dioxide.

**Process:**

1. Organic waste is placed in an anaerobic digester.
2. Microorganisms break down the organic material.
3. Biogas is produced.
4. The biogas can be collected and used as an energy source.
5. The remaining digestate may be used as a soil amendment if it meets safety requirements.

**Advantages:**

- Uses organic waste.
- Produces a useful energy source.
- Can reduce uncontrolled methane release.
- Recycles nutrients.

### D. WASTEWATER TREATMENT

Wastewater treatment removes or reduces contaminants before water is discharged or reused.

**General stages:**

1. **Preliminary treatment**

   Screens and grit-removal systems remove large objects and sand.

2. **Primary treatment**

   Suspended solids settle or are removed through physical processes.

3. **Secondary treatment**

   Microorganisms break down biodegradable organic matter.

4. **Tertiary or advanced treatment**

   Additional processes remove remaining nutrients, pathogens or other pollutants when required.

5. **Disinfection**

   Suitable methods such as ultraviolet treatment, chlorine or ozone may reduce disease-causing microorganisms.

**Importance:**

- Protects rivers and other water bodies.
- Reduces the spread of waterborne diseases.
- Improves water quality.
- Supports safe water reuse where appropriate.

## 10.4.7 Green Technology in Agriculture

Green technology can make agricultural production more efficient while reducing environmental damage.

**Examples:**

1. **Drip irrigation**

   Delivers water close to plant roots.

   Benefits:
   - Reduces water wastage.
   - Improves water-use efficiency.
   - Can reduce unnecessary wetting of surrounding soil.

2. **Precision agriculture**

   Uses sensors, GPS, data and other technologies to manage agricultural inputs accurately.

   Benefits:
   - Helps apply fertilisers and water where needed.
   - Reduces unnecessary chemical use.
   - Improves resource efficiency.

3. **Biological pest control**

   Uses natural predators, parasites or pathogens to control pests.

   Benefits:
   - Can reduce dependence on chemical pesticides.
   - May protect beneficial organisms when properly managed.

4. **Controlled-environment agriculture**

   Uses systems such as greenhouses or indoor farms to control growing conditions.

   Benefits:
   - Allows more precise control of water and nutrients.
   - Can improve production efficiency.
   - May reduce certain pest problems.

## 10.4.8 Advantages and Limitations of Green Technology

**Advantages:**

1. Reduces pollution.
2. Improves energy efficiency.
3. Reduces dependence on non-renewable resources.
4. Helps reduce greenhouse gas emissions.
5. Encourages sustainable development.
6. Promotes innovation and green industries.
7. Can reduce long-term operating costs.

**Limitations:**

1. High initial investment.
2. Some technologies depend on weather conditions.
3. Certain technologies require specialised maintenance.
4. Manufacturing may require scarce raw materials.
5. Waste from old equipment must be managed properly.
6. Not every technology is suitable for every location.

## 10.4.9 Importance of Green Technology in Sustainable Development

Sustainable development balances three important dimensions:

1. Environmental protection.
2. Economic development.
3. Social well-being.

Green technology contributes by:

- Reducing environmental damage.
- Supporting cleaner industries.
- Improving access to efficient energy.
- Creating employment in green sectors.
- Supporting better waste and water management.
- Improving quality of life.

Green technology should be selected based on its full environmental impact, cost, suitability and long-term benefits.

# EXAM TIPS AND COMMON MISTAKES

## Important Exam Tips

1. When explaining eutrophication, include excess nutrients, algal bloom, reduced light penetration, decomposition and reduced dissolved oxygen.

2. When explaining global warming, connect increased greenhouse gas concentrations to greater absorption and re-emission of infrared radiation.

3. When explaining acid rain, identify sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides as important precursor gases.

4. When explaining biomagnification, describe increasing pollutant concentration at higher trophic levels.

5. When comparing in situ and ex situ conservation, state where conservation takes place and give an example of each.

6. When explaining the 5R concept, give the correct meaning and a relevant example for each R.

7. When describing green technology, explain how it reduces environmental impact rather than merely naming the technology.

8. When evaluating renewable energy, include both advantages and limitations.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1:** Saying that the greenhouse effect is entirely harmful.

Correction: The natural greenhouse effect is essential for maintaining Earth's temperature. The enhanced greenhouse effect contributes to global warming.

**Mistake 2:** Saying that all pollutants undergo biomagnification.

Correction: Biomagnification is associated with certain persistent pollutants that accumulate in organisms and become more concentrated at higher trophic levels.

**Mistake 3:** Confusing bioaccumulation and biomagnification.

Correction: Bioaccumulation occurs within an organism over time. Biomagnification occurs across trophic levels.

**Mistake 4:** Saying that eutrophication happens because algae directly consume all the oxygen.

Correction: Decomposers breaking down dead organic matter use dissolved oxygen, contributing to oxygen depletion.

**Mistake 5:** Confusing preservation and restoration.

Correction: Preservation protects existing ecosystems, while restoration helps damaged ecosystems recover.

**Mistake 6:** Saying that recycling and reusing are the same.

Correction: Reusing means using an item again, while recycling involves processing waste into new materials.

**Mistake 7:** Claiming that electric vehicles have zero environmental impact.

Correction: EVs have no tailpipe emissions, but battery production, electricity generation and disposal can have environmental impacts.

**Mistake 8:** Assuming all renewable energy sources are completely harmless.

Correction: Renewable energy sources have environmental impacts that must be managed.

# SPM-STYLE PRACTICE QUESTIONS

## QUESTION 1 — 4 MARKS

State four human activities that can threaten the environment.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Deforestation.
2. Open burning.
3. Excessive use of chemical fertilisers.
4. Discharge of untreated industrial waste.

## QUESTION 2 — 4 MARKS

Explain how deforestation can contribute to environmental problems.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Deforestation destroys natural habitats.
2. This reduces the population of organisms and biodiversity.
3. Removal of tree roots exposes soil to erosion.
4. Fewer trees are available to absorb carbon dioxide through photosynthesis.

## QUESTION 3 — 5 MARKS

Explain the formation of acid rain.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Burning fossil fuels releases sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
2. These gases enter the atmosphere.
3. They react with oxygen and water through atmospheric chemical reactions.
4. Sulphuric acid and nitric acid can form.
5. These acids enter precipitation and fall to Earth's surface.

## QUESTION 4 — 6 MARKS

Explain how eutrophication causes the death of aquatic organisms.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Excess nitrates and phosphates enter a water body.
2. Algae reproduce rapidly and form an algal bloom.
3. The algal bloom reduces light penetration.
4. Submerged aquatic plants receive less light and may die.
5. Decomposers break down dead organic matter and use dissolved oxygen.
6. The dissolved oxygen level decreases, causing aquatic organisms to die.

## QUESTION 5 — 4 MARKS

Differentiate between bioaccumulation and biomagnification.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Bioaccumulation is the gradual accumulation of a pollutant within an organism over time.
2. It occurs when absorption exceeds elimination.
3. Biomagnification is the increase in pollutant concentration at successive trophic levels.
4. It occurs when predators consume contaminated prey.

## QUESTION 6 — 4 MARKS

Differentiate between preservation and conservation.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Preservation protects ecosystems from harmful human interference.
2. It aims to maintain existing natural conditions.
3. Conservation involves responsible management and sustainable use of natural resources.
4. It aims to prevent resource depletion and maintain biodiversity.

## QUESTION 7 — 4 MARKS

Differentiate between in situ and ex situ conservation.

**Suggested answer:**

1. In situ conservation takes place within an organism's natural habitat.
2. Examples include national parks and wildlife sanctuaries.
3. Ex situ conservation takes place outside an organism's natural habitat.
4. Examples include zoos, botanical gardens and seed banks.

## QUESTION 8 — 5 MARKS

Explain five practices in the 5R concept.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Refuse means avoiding unnecessary products.
2. Reduce means using fewer resources.
3. Reuse means using an item again instead of discarding it.
4. Recycle means processing waste materials into new products.
5. Recover means obtaining useful energy or materials from waste.

## QUESTION 9 — 5 MARKS

Explain how sustainable agriculture helps protect the environment.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Crop rotation helps maintain soil fertility.
2. Organic fertilisers add organic matter to the soil.
3. Integrated pest management reduces unnecessary pesticide use.
4. Contour farming slows surface runoff.
5. Terracing reduces soil erosion on slopes.

## QUESTION 10 — 4 MARKS

Describe how solar panels generate electricity.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Sunlight reaches photovoltaic cells.
2. Semiconductor material absorbs light energy.
3. The absorbed energy releases charge carriers.
4. Their movement produces an electric current.

## QUESTION 11 — 4 MARKS

State two advantages and two limitations of wind energy.

**Suggested answer:**

Advantages:

1. Wind is a renewable energy source.
2. Wind turbines generate electricity without direct fuel combustion.

Limitations:

3. Electricity production depends on wind availability.
4. Wind turbines may affect birds and bats if poorly located.

## QUESTION 12 — 5 MARKS

Explain how biogas technology contributes to environmental sustainability.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Organic waste is placed in an anaerobic digester.
2. Microorganisms break down organic matter without oxygen.
3. Biogas containing methane is produced.
4. The biogas can be used to generate heat or electricity.
5. The technology makes useful use of organic waste and can reduce uncontrolled methane emissions.

## QUESTION 13 — 6 MARKS

Discuss the importance of green technology in environmental sustainability.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Green technology reduces environmental pollution.
2. Renewable energy technology reduces dependence on fossil fuels.
3. Energy-efficient systems reduce energy consumption.
4. Waste-management technology reduces the amount of waste sent to landfills.
5. Water-treatment technology reduces the release of pollutants into water bodies.
6. Green technology supports sustainable development and improves quality of life.

## QUESTION 14 — 6 MARKS

A river near an agricultural area experiences frequent algal blooms. Suggest measures to reduce this problem.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Control the application of chemical fertilisers.
2. Avoid applying fertilisers immediately before heavy rainfall.
3. Establish vegetation buffer zones along riverbanks.
4. Treat sewage before discharge.
5. Prevent animal waste from entering the river.
6. Monitor nutrient concentrations in the water.

## QUESTION 15 — 8 MARKS

Discuss how individuals and governments can work together to achieve environmental sustainability.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Individuals can practise the 5R concept to reduce waste.
2. Individuals can conserve water and electricity.
3. Communities can participate in tree-planting programmes.
4. Individuals can use public transportation to reduce emissions.
5. Governments can enforce environmental protection laws.
6. Governments can establish protected areas to conserve biodiversity.
7. Governments can promote renewable energy and green technology.
8. Governments can provide environmental education and support sustainable development programmes.
